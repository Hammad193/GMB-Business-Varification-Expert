Storefront Verification Questions

Find answers to common questions about storefront verification and physical location approval on Google Maps.

Storefront verification is the Google Business Profile approval process for physical retail locations that requires displaying a customer facing address and business signage.

Storefront verification requires a visible physical location with signage and customer visits, while SAB verification allows businesses to serve customers without displaying an address.

Retail shops, restaurants, salons, offices, medical clinics, grocery stores, and any business with a physical location open to customers needs storefront verification.

Required documents include business license, lease agreement, property proof, signage photos, utility bills, and evidence of physical store operations and customer visits.

Storefront verification typically takes 3 to 14 days, including postcard delivery time, video verification review, and Google's address confirmation process.

Google requires evidence like storefront signage photos, interior location shots, operational proof, utility bills, and any documentation showing legitimate physical presence.

Storefront verification often fails due to incomplete documentation, mismatched business name to signage, address inconsistencies, or Google's policy compliance issues.

You can start the verification process but cannot complete it without evidence of active operations like signage, interior setup, and a ready to serve location.

If rejected, you can appeal by providing additional documentation, correcting profile information, and demonstrating that your physical location is legitimate and active.

Storefront verification is essential for ranking as it signals to Google that your physical location is real, building trust and improving map pack visibility.

Video verification is increasingly important for storefront approval as it provides real time evidence of business operations, signage, and customer serving environment.
